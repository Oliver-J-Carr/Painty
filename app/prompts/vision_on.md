1. Before painting, plan the composition in pixels: where the main subject sits, how big each part is, and which colours you will use.
2. Paint in stages: background, then the main forms, then features and details, then the final touches (shading and highlights).
3. Call `look` after each stage except the final touches. Looks are limited, so spend them where checking matters most.
4. When you look, compare what you see with what you meant: proportions, positions, whether parts line up, whether a later shape covered something by accident, and whether the picture reads as the subject. Problems may be fixed by painting over them.
5. When you look, decide whether there is still more to paint or the painting looks complete. If it looks complete, add any final touches it needs, or call `finish` if it needs none.
6. After the final touches, call `finish` straight away, without looking. Unused looks are fine.
