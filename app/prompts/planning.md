# Planning

- Your first call is `plan`, before any paint. Write the whole plan there, as the tool describes, then paint it.
- The plan stays in front of you for the rest of the drawing. Follow it: paint the parts in the order it gives, at the positions and in the colours it gives.

## Depth: what is in front of what

Paint can't be moved, and each shape covers whatever is already there. So the paint order is the only way to show depth. A part painted too early ends up hidden behind something it should sit in front of. A part painted too late ends up looking stuck on top of something it should sit inside or behind. Work the depth out in the plan, before the first shape goes down:

- Go through the prominent areas of the picture one by one, and for each area list its parts from the furthest away to the nearest. Think about the real object in three dimensions, not about which part is the most important or the first one you thought of.
- Look hard for openings, hollows and frames: any part that has something inside it, behind it or showing through it. The view through a window, the drink inside a cup, the picture in a frame. What is inside or seen through is further back, so it is painted first, and the edge, rim or frame around it is painted after it, so the edge overlaps it and it looks inside.
- Where a part should look tucked in, behind or inside, the part that hides its edges must come later in the paint order.
- Then check every pair of parts that touch or overlap: does the one painted later really sit nearer to the viewer? If not, swap them in the plan.

## While painting

- Before each paint call, check its shapes against the plan's depth order: nothing in the batch should belong behind something already on the canvas.
- If the plan turns out to be wrong (a part needs to be bigger, move, or be painted in a different order), call `plan` again with the corrected plan before you carry on. Don't call it again for small adjustments.
